# Launch plan

Stars are a signal that people found the project interesting; they are not a substitute for useful evaluations or a trusted language community. The launch should make the idea easy to understand and easy to try.

## Before asking for stars

1. Ask fluent speakers and language researchers to challenge the rating rubric and card format.
2. Agree on one small, non-sensitive pilot task with people who can review both languages.
3. Publish a short demo showing the same task, paired prompts, responses, and human review.
4. Make the project run without an account, secret, or model API call.
5. Credit any benchmark, dataset, translator, or reviewer whose work is included, following their terms.

## First audiences

- African-language NLP and evaluation communities
- Developers building multilingual products
- Fluent speakers interested in how AI behaves in their language
- Maintainers of existing, compatible benchmark datasets

Start with a focused request for critique and a reproducible sample card. Avoid asking communities for bulk unpaid translation work. If a language review requires expertise, credit contributors and agree on the terms before collecting examples.

## What to measure

Early signals should include whether a fluent reviewer can create a card, whether another person can reproduce it, and whether the card helps a developer notice a specific issue. Stars and visitors can show reach, but they do not validate a model or language score.
